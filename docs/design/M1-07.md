# M1-07 — `@liminal/ui`: tokens and the first seven components

> Design brief for card [M1-07](../specs/M1-sound/M1-07.md). Written against
> [principles.md](principles.md) and [components.md](components.md). Every number here is meant to
> be implemented literally; where a number is missing, ask instead of inventing.

## Job

**One line:** give every screen of liminal the same handful of controls, so that a person always
finds play, the numbers and the way out of an error in the same place, with the same look and the
same keys.

This card ships no product behaviour of its own. It serves **F0** (set up the sound), **F1** (open →
music) and **F5** (recover), because the controls it defines are what those flows are made of:
`Transport` and `Readout` are hierarchy level 1 in the principles, `ErrorStrip` is F5's whole
surface, and `Button` · `Toggle` · `Slider` · `Select` are what M1-04 needs to put volume, mute and
the output device on screen.

The screen this brief touches directly is the M0-02 shell, today an empty `<main />`. After M1-07 it
shows the transport and the numbers, at rest, and nothing else (§9).

> The card carries no `design_review: true`, and this brief introduces no new product screen — it
> defines the system the screens are made of. No `design: ok` gate is claimed here. If the loop can
> publish one, a design canvas of the seven components at 1440 would be useful for the review round;
> see Open questions › Q5.

failures in a status bar or a modal; ours is a strip in the user's words with exactly one action,
because F5 says recovery is one click and no modal may cover the transport.

### 1.2 New tokens proposed, and why

`principles.md` names nine colour roles. Three more are proposed here, by role, never by value; if
the owner prefers to keep the list at nine, see Open questions › Q1.

| Token | Role | Why it cannot be one of the nine |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-surface-3` | the transient fill of a control under the pointer or pressed | `surface-2` is already the panel; hover on a panel needs a third step, and reusing `surface-2` makes hover invisible on a panel. |
| `--color-line-strong` | the edge of a control that must be identifiable (1.4.11) | `--color-line` at 1.25:1 is the right weight for a separator and illegal as a control border. Two jobs, two tokens. |
| `--color-signal-error-ink` | text on an error fill (`Button variant="danger"`) | `accent-ink` is tuned to `accent`; on the error fill it fails. |
| `--color-accent-hover` / `--color-accent-active` | the accent's own two interaction steps | A filled accent control cannot express hover with a surface token. |
| `--color-focus` | the focus ring | Aliased to `accent` today; a separate name lets the ring change without moving the accent. |

Sizing, motion and type tokens above (`--control-height-*`, `--hit-min`, `--focus-ring-*`,
`--tempo-bpm`) are new by role for the same reason: a component must never write `36px`.

### 1.3 Type, in words

- **Display** (`--font-display`): headings and numbers that carry meaning. No download: it resolves
  to Avenir Next on macOS, to the platform UI face on Linux, `system-ui` last. Character comes from
  weight `--weight-semibold` and `--tracking-tight`, never from a downloaded face.
- **Body** (`--font-body`): every label, button and sentence, `--weight-regular`, `--leading-normal`.
- **Mono** (`--font-mono`): every value that aligns — tempo, key, `bar:beat`, elapsed, dB, sizes.
  Always with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`.
- **Scale**: fixed ratio **1.2**, floor **13 px** (`--text-0`), which is the smallest text the
  principles allow. `--text-1` 15 is the body size; the shell sets it on `<html>`.
- **Nothing below `--text-0`.** A component that wants smaller text is wrong.

### 1.4 Motion, in words

| Where | Property | Duration | Easing |
|---|---|---|---|
| hover and focus on any control | `background-color`, `border-color`, `color`, `outline-color` | `--duration-1` | `--ease-out` |
| a state change that stays (toggle pressed, transport state, select open) | `background-color`, `transform`, `opacity` | `--duration-2` | `--ease-out` entering, `--ease-in-out` moving |
| the slider thumb growing while dragged | `transform` | `--duration-1` | `--ease-out` |
| the beat tick in `Transport` | `opacity` 1 → 0.35 | `--duration-beat` | `--ease-out` |
| `ErrorStrip` entering | `opacity` 0 → 1, `translateY` −4px → 0 | `--duration-2` | `--ease-out` |

`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` sets `--duration-1` and `--duration-2` to 1 ms, which turns every
transition off, and **the beat tick loses its animation and becomes a static indicator**: filled
while playing, hollow otherwise — exactly what the principles ask. No component may animate with a
hard-coded duration, so the media query is the single switch.

## 2. Rules that apply to all seven

**Focus ring — one rule, no exceptions.**

```css
.control:focus-visible {
  outline: var(--focus-ring-width) solid var(--color-focus);
  outline-offset: var(--focus-ring-offset);
  border-radius: inherit;
}
```

`:focus-visible`, never `:focus`; `outline`, never `box-shadow`, so the ring sits on whatever
surface is behind and always clears 3:1 (§1.1). Nothing sets `outline: none` without replacing it in
the same rule. The ring is never clipped: any ancestor that would clip it uses `padding` of at least
`--space-1`, never `overflow: hidden`.

**Hit targets.** Minimum **24 × 24 px** for any pointer target (SC 2.5.8), and our own floor is
higher: **28 px** (`--control-height-sm`) for anything in a dense row, **36 px** for a control on
the deck bar, **44 px** for the transport's play/pause. Where the visible shape is smaller than the
target — the slider thumb — the hit area is enlarged with a transparent `::before` of
`--hit-min` square, centred; the visible thumb keeps its size.

**Disabled — one treatment.** Never opacity. A disabled control uses `--color-muted` for text and
icon, `--color-line` for its border, `--color-surface-2` for a filled variant, and
`cursor: not-allowed`. Filled variants lose their accent entirely, so disabled never reads as
actionable. A control that is disabled because something is missing carries `disabledReason`, a
sentence in the user's words, rendered as visually hidden text and wired with `aria-describedby`,
so "why can I not press this" is answerable by screen reader and by the consumer's own hint line.
`Button` uses the real `disabled` attribute; a **loading** button instead keeps focus and uses
`aria-disabled="true"` with a click guard, because losing focus mid-action is worse than a dead
click.

**Tokens only.** No literal colour, length, font, duration or easing anywhere in
`packages/ui/src` outside `tokens.css`, and none left in `apps/desktop`. `1px` borders come from
`--border-width`; `1ch` widths in `Readout` are the one arithmetic exception and are named
(`--readout-tempo-ch` …).

**Every component takes** `className?: string` and `id?: string` and forwards its ref; nothing takes
a boolean positional parameter; a new variant is always a **new prop whose default preserves what
exists**.

## 3. `Button`

**Anatomy.** `[ optional icon 16 ][ gap --control-gap ][ label ][ gap ][ optional icon 16 ]` inside
a box of `--control-height-*`, horizontal padding `--control-pad-*`, `--radius-2`, border
`--border-width` solid, `--font-body`, `--weight-medium`, `--leading-snug`, text centred.

**Props**

| Prop | Values | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `variant` | `'primary' \| 'quiet' \| 'danger'` | `'quiet'` | quiet is the everyday button; one primary per region |
| `size` | `'sm' \| 'md' \| 'lg'` | `'md'` | 28 / 36 / 44 px tall |
| `iconStart` | `ReactNode` | — | 16 px square, `aria-hidden` |
| `iconEnd` | `ReactNode` | — | 16 px square, `aria-hidden` |
| `iconOnly` | `boolean` | `false` | square box; `label` becomes the accessible name only |
| `label` | `string` | required | the accessible name; also the visible text unless `iconOnly` |
| `loading` | `boolean` | `false` | keeps focus, blocks activation |
| `busyLabel` | `string` | `'Working…'` | replaces the visible label while loading |
| `disabled` | `boolean` | `false` | real `disabled` attribute |
| `disabledReason` | `string` | — | visually hidden, `aria-describedby` |
| `asChild` | `boolean` | `false` | Radix `Slot`, for a link that looks like a button |
| `onClick` | `(event) => void` | — | |

**No `type` prop.** The element is always `type="button"`. There is no form anywhere in the product,
so `type="submit"` is a value no screen reaches and no story can honestly show. The day a form
lands, the prop comes back as a new optional prop whose default is `'button'`, which preserves what
every consumer has today.

| `md` | 36 | `--space-3` 12 | `--text-1` 15 | 16 | 80 | 36 × 36 |
| `lg` | 44 | `--space-4` 16 | `--text-2` 18 | 20 | 96 | 44 × 44 |

## 7. `Readout`

Tempo · key · `bar:beat` · elapsed. Display only: no click, no hover, no focus. The numbers change
under your eyes and **nothing may move**.

**Anatomy.** A horizontal group of four fields, each `[ label ]` over `[ value ]`, separated by a
1 px vertical rule of `--color-line`, height 24, with `--space-5` (24) on each side of the rule.

| Part | Size and tokens |
|---|---|
| group | `role="group"`, `aria-label="Now playing"`, background transparent, height **56 px fixed** |
| label | `--text-0` 13, `--font-body`, `--color-muted`, `text-transform: uppercase`, `--tracking-wide`, `--leading-normal` → 20 px tall |
| gap | `--space-1` 4 |
| value | `--font-mono`, `--color-ink`, `--leading-tight`, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` |
| value size, primary fields (tempo, key, `bar:beat`) | `--text-4` 26 → 30 px tall |
| value size, secondary field (elapsed) | `--text-2` 18, `--color-muted` |
| unit ("BPM") | `--text-0` 13, `--color-muted`, `--space-1` to the left of the number, baseline-aligned |
| the live number | tempo's digits use `--color-accent` while `playing` is true; otherwise `--color-ink` |

**No layout shift — exactly how.** Four things together, all four required:

1. `--font-mono` on every value, so every digit has the same advance;
2. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, which also fixes the advance of the faces that ship
   proportional figures in a monospace family;
3. **each value is an `inline-block` with a fixed width in `ch` and `text-align: right`** — the box
   is sized for the widest value the field can ever hold, so adding a digit fills the reserved space
   instead of growing the box:
   `--readout-tempo-ch: 3` ("128") · `--readout-key-ch: 6` ("F# min") · `--readout-bar-ch: 7`
   ("99999:4") · `--readout-elapsed-ch: 7` ("9:59:59", and "12:34" right-aligned inside it);
4. the group has a **fixed 56 px height** and each field a fixed width of
   `calc(<ch token> * 1ch + <unit width>)`, so a missing value renders an em dash `—` in the same
   box and the row does not reflow.

The proof is not a jsdom test — the test DOM computes no layout. It is the Playwright measurement in
the card's evidence: render tempo 99 → 100 → 128, bar 9:4 → 10:1, elapsed 9:59 → 10:00, and assert
each field's `getBoundingClientRect()` is byte-identical across the three renders.

**Props**

| Prop | Values | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tempo` | `number \| null` | `null` | rounded to integer BPM; `null` → `—` |
| `musicalKey` | `string \| null` | `null` | e.g. `'F# min'`; `null` → `—` |
| `bar` | `number \| null` | `null` | 1-based |
| `beat` | `number \| null` | `null` | 1-based; rendered as `bar:beat` |
| `elapsedMs` | `number \| null` | `null` | `m:ss` under an hour, `h:mm:ss` above |
| `playing` | `boolean` | `false` | only effect: the tempo digits take `--color-accent` |
| `size` | `'md' \| 'lg'` | `'md'` | `lg`: primary values `--text-5` 31, group height 64 |
| `labels` | `{ tempo: string; key: string; position: string; elapsed: string }` | `{ tempo: 'Tempo', key: 'Key', position: 'Bar', elapsed: 'Elapsed' }` | the words on screen |

**Stories.** `Empty`, `Stopped`, `Playing`, `PlayingLongValues` (tempo 128, bar 1024:3, elapsed

Play / pause / stop. The one control that must always be one click away; no modal ever covers it.

**Anatomy.** A row: `[ play-pause lg ][ gap --space-2 ][ stop 44×44 icon-only ][ gap --space-4 ][

| Part | Build | Size |

## 9. `ErrorStrip`

What went wrong, in the user's words, and the one thing to do about it. F5.

**Anatomy.** `[ 3px left rule ][ icon 16 ][ gap --space-3 ][ title over optional detail ][ spacer ][
action Button quiet sm ][ gap --space-2 ][ dismiss 28×28 icon-only ]`.

| Part | Tokens and size |
|---|---|
| box | background `--color-surface-2`, border `--border-width` `--color-line-strong`, `--radius-2`, padding `--space-3` `--space-4`, min-height 48, full width of the content column |
| left rule | 3 px, `--color-signal-error` (or `--color-signal-warn` when `tone="warn"`), full height, `--radius-2` on the left corners |
| icon | 16 px, same colour as the rule, `aria-hidden` |
| title | `--text-1` 15, `--font-body`, `--weight-medium`, `--color-ink`, `--leading-snug` |
| detail | `--text-0` 13, `--color-muted`, `--leading-normal`, `--space-1` under the title |
| action | `Button variant="quiet" size="sm"` |
| dismiss | `Button variant="quiet" size="sm" iconOnly label="Dismiss"` |

**Copy rules.** The title is one sentence in the listener's words, naming what happened, never a
code, never a stack: "We could not reach the audio device." The action is a verb the person
recognises: "Try again", "Choose another device", "Use the example set". The detail line is optional
and carries at most one extra sentence. No "Error:", no "Something went wrong".

**Props**

| Prop | Values | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `title` | `string` | required | the sentence |
| `detail` | `string` | — | one more sentence, at most |
| `tone` | `'error' \| 'warn'` | `'error'` | warn for something degraded but working |
| `action` | `{ label: string; onAction: () => void }` | — | exactly one; the type takes no array |
| `dismissal` | `{ onDismiss: () => void; focusOnDismiss: RefObject<HTMLElement \| null> }` | — | absent → no dismiss button; both fields required together |

**Dismissal is one prop, not two.** The handler and the place focus goes are a single decision: the
strip is dismissed by pressing a button inside the strip, and that button unmounts with it, so
something must say where focus lands. Two independent props let a consumer offer the close button
and stay silent about focus, and then focus falls to `<body>` — the whole app loses its place. Put
in one required-together object, the type refuses that combination at compile time.

**States.** rest (title only) · with detail · with action · with action and dismiss · `warn` tone ·
entering (opacity and 4 px rise over `--duration-2`) · dismissed (unmounts; `--duration-1` fade).
There is no hover state on the box itself; only the two buttons react.

**Keyboard.** Focus order: action, then dismiss. `Esc` **does not** dismiss: the strip is not a
dialog, and `Esc` inside the app belongs to whatever is open. Dismissal **always** moves focus to
`dismissal.focusOnDismiss`, and the component focuses that element before it calls `onDismiss`, so
there is no frame in which nothing is focused. "Focus stays where it was" is not an option here:
where it was is the dismiss button, and that button is what unmounts. A consumer that has no
sensible place to send focus should not offer a dismiss button at all.

**Screen reader.** `role="alert"` on the box (assertive by definition, and it does not move focus),
`aria-atomic="true"` so title and detail are read as one message. The icon is `aria-hidden`; the
tone is carried by the words, never by the colour of the rule. The strip is rendered **only when
there is an error** — an always-mounted empty `role="alert"` swallows the next announcement.

**Stories.** `TitleOnly`, `WithDetail`, `WithAction`, `WithActionAndDismiss`, `WarnTone`,
`LongTitleWrapping` (two-line title at 1024, proving the box grows and the buttons stay aligned to
the top), `DeviceLost` (the real F5 copy: "We could not reach the audio device." / "Try again"),
`KeyboardDismiss` (`play`: `Tab` to the action, `Enter`; `Tab` to dismiss, `Enter`; assert
`onDismiss` and that focus went to `focusOnDismiss`).

## 10. The M0-02 shell after this card

Today `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/App.tsx` is `<main />`. After M1-07 it composes `Transport` and
`Readout` and nothing else. **No data is wired in this card** — M1-04 brings the engine, the volume,
the device and the error. So the shell renders the rest state honestly: `Transport state="stopped"`,
`Readout` with every value `null` (four em dashes). It renders **no placeholder copy and no example
set**; the F1 empty state is M1-04's, and inventing it here would be inventing behaviour.

**Play at rest is disabled, and says why.** The shell passes `canPlay={false}` with
`disabledReason="There is nothing to play yet."` — written here, and the one line of copy this
card owns.

Why not an enabled Play. There is no engine behind this shell, so an enabled Play is a button that
looks like the most important control in the app and does nothing when pressed. That is the false
affordance the principles call dishonest and heuristic 1 of `usability.md` forbids ("feedback within
100 ms of any action"). Making it do something instead — an example set, a first-run line — is F1
behaviour that belongs to M1-04, and would be inventing behaviour here. Between a control that lies
and a control that states its condition, the honest one is the disabled one. `Transport` already had
`canPlay` and `disabledReason` for exactly this (§8); the shell is their first consumer.

Why the sentence is design's to own, not invented product copy. `disabledReason` renders as hidden
text bound with `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader speaks it, which makes it product copy — and
that is precisely why it is written here rather than left to the implementation. It says the state
of a control, not what the app is or what it will do; it names no feature, promises no example set,
and it is replaced, not added to, when M1-04 wires the engine and Play becomes real. Wording rules:
the listener's words, one sentence, no "Error", no "not implemented", no jargon. "There is nothing
to play yet." passes; "No score loaded." and "Engine not connected." do not.

The cost is that at rest the deck bar holds **no tabbable control** — both Play and Stop are
disabled — which §11 now states plainly instead of promising a tab order the screen does not have.
A screen reader still reaches Play by reading the screen and hears the reason, so "why can I not
press this" stays answerable; only `Tab` skips it. That emptiness is the truth about a shell with
nothing wired to it, and it disappears in M1-04, when `canPlay` becomes true and the tab order in
§11 is the one that was always intended.

**Gutters and widths**

| Width | Deck bar padding-inline | Content column | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | `--space-5` 24 | fluid, gutters 24 | `Transport` (approx. 300) + `Readout` (approx. 420) + gutters = approx. 768, so the bar never wraps |
| 1440 | `--space-6` 32 | fluid, gutters 32 | |

The two numbers the shell owns are tokens like everything else — `--shell-deck-height` (the height
of the deck bar) and `--shell-content-max` (the widest the content column ever gets) — so the shell
never writes `88px` or `1200px` of its own. Otherwise it keeps **no local token and no local
style**: `tokens.css` comes from `@liminal/ui`, and the app's own copy is deleted, not emptied.

## 11. Keyboard and accessibility, across the seven

- **Every flow by keyboard.** The tab order of the deck bar is: play/pause → stop → (error action →
  dismiss when a strip is present) → end. `Readout` is never in the tab order.
- **In the shell of this card the tab order is empty**, and that is deliberate (§10): nothing is
  wired, so Play is disabled with a reason and Stop is disabled because nothing is playing, and a
  disabled `<button>` is not tabbable. There is no flow to complete here and no control that a
  keyboard user is kept away from. Both buttons carry their reason as text a screen reader reads
  while browsing the screen. The order above becomes live in M1-04, with no change to `Transport`.
- **Focus is always visible** (§2) and never obscured (SC 2.4.11): the deck bar is not sticky over
  anything in this card, and the `Select` panel is portalled above everything.
- **No information by colour alone**, anywhere: `Transport` carries a word and a shape, `Select`
  marks the selected item with a check, `Toggle` with a filled or hollow dot, `ErrorStrip` with a
  sentence and an icon, `Readout` with labels.
- **Live regions**: exactly two in this card — the `Transport` state chip (`polite`) and
  `ErrorStrip` (`role="alert"`). Nothing that changes on the beat is announced.
- **Names**: every control has a visible label or, when icon-only, an accessible name from `label`.
- **Reduced motion** is a single media query in `tokens.css` plus the `Transport` pulse rule.
- **Text spacing** (SC 1.4.12): no fixed-height box holds wrapping text; the fixed heights in this
  brief (`Readout` 56, deck bar 88, chip 28) hold single-line content whose size is token-driven, so
  a 200 % `line-height` override still fits inside the 88 px bar at `--text-1`. `ErrorStrip` grows.
- **Zoom** (SC 1.4.4): at 200 % zoom at 1024 the deck bar wraps `Readout` below `Transport` rather
  than clipping; give the bar `flex-wrap: wrap` and `min-height: 88px` instead of `height`.
- **`axe` passes with zero violations** on every story, `addon-a11y` on.

## 13. Open questions

**Q1 — the five colour tokens beyond the nine roles** (`surface-3`, `line-strong`,
`signal-error-ink`, `accent-hover`, `accent-active`, `focus`).
(a) adopt them and add the rows to `principles.md › Tokens` in this PR — recommended, each has a
distinct job that no existing role covers; (b) keep the nine and derive the rest with
`color-mix()` in the components — fewer names but a loose value in disguise, and hover on a panel
becomes invisible; (c) keep the nine and drop hover feedback on quiet controls — rejected, it fails
"visible state within 100 ms".

**Q2 — `Button`'s default variant.** (a) `quiet`, as written — a booth full of primary buttons is
noise, and the one primary per region is a deliberate choice; (b) `primary`, matching most design
systems, at the cost of accidental emphasis.

**Q3 — `Transport` and the `Space` key.** (a) the component binds nothing and M1-04 owns the global
shortcut, as written — safe when a prompt box exists; (b) the component binds `Space` on a container
with `tabIndex`, which is fewer moving parts now and a bug the day a text field lands.

**Q4 — the shell's stage in M1-07.** (a) genuinely empty until M1-04, as written; (b) a single line
of copy saying what will happen — closer to "the empty state is a state", but it is F1 copy and
belongs to the card that owns F1.

**Q5 — a design canvas.** If the loop can publish one, a canvas of the seven components in both
themes at 1440 would let a reviewer compare the built Storybook against a picture instead of against
prose. Offered, not required; the numbered measurements in §12 are the real gate.

**Q6 — a display face with more character.** The stack in §1.3 resolves to system faces, so numbers
get their character from weight and tracking only. If a downloaded face is ever acceptable, that is
a course decision (bundle size, licence, offline install), not a design one.
