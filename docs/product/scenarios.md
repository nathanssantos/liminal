# Scenarios the loop walks

> Short stories of a person using liminal. The loop walks them with Playwright at every
> discovery iteration and every release, as that person, and writes down where the story breaks
> — a missing control, a silent wait, a word that does not make sense. A broken step becomes an
> `idea` (or a card, if it is inside the open milestone). New scenarios come from research,
> from issues people open, and from what the loop itself notices; they are added by PR.

## Listener, first time (F0, F1, F1½)

1. Opens the app. Sees what it is and one obvious thing to do.
2. Presses play without reading anything. Hears music at a level that is not too loud.
3. Reaches for the volume. Finds it in one glance; keyboard works.
4. It is a bit bright; wants it warmer. Finds the filter or a layer to turn off.
4b. Wants the lead softer. Opens the layer map, finds the lead by soloing it, picks a pluck, and
   sees "next phrase, in 5 bars".
5. Wants to know what is playing. Reads key, tempo, section, and what comes next.
6. Pastes a link of a track they like. Sees it analyzing, then sees the set start moving there.
7. Closes the app; opens it tomorrow. Volume and device are as they left them.

## Listener at a party (F2, F2b, F1½)

1. The set is playing. Someone asks for "more energy". Two seconds later it is on its way, and
   the screen says when.
2. Wants to hold the current section a bit longer. One control.
3. Queues three links for the next hour, gives the second one 20 minutes.
4. A link fails. Sees why and retries; the set never stops.
5. Something sounded great. "Keep that" saves the last minutes.

## DJ (F1½, F2, F4)

1. Wants to pre-listen to where the set is going. Cue on headphones.
2. Kills the bass for a phrase, brings it back on the one.
3. Sweeps the filter into a break; the beat keeps.
4. Nudges the tempo up two BPM over a couple of tracks.
5. Locks the key for a while; releases it.
6. Asks "why did it do that?" and reads an answer they believe.
7. Rebuilds a track from the layer map: new bass pattern, hats shuffled, pad out of the drop —
   and locks the lead they like.

## Producer (F7, F6)

1. Writes a brief, pastes a reference, gets a full track in a minute.
2. Says "more bass in the drop". Sees, in words, what changed and where.
3. Likes the drop; locks it. Regenerates the intro twice.
4. Compares two versions; goes back to the first.
5. Exports a wav; later, stems and MIDI. Opens the project again next week.

## Contributor (open source)

1. Reads the README, understands what it is in five minutes, runs it.
2. Finds how to propose an idea and how the loop treats it.
3. Opens a bug with the template; sees it labelled and answered.
